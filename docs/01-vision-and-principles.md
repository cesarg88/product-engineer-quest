# Vision & Principles

## 1. What is The Product Engineer Quest?

The Product Engineer Quest, or PEQ, is a structured RPG-inspired professional development system designed to help software engineers evolve into Product Engineers.

PEQ combines professional growth, product thinking, business awareness, communication, leadership, and AI-native engineering into a clear progression system based on quests, skills, XP, evidence, and reflection.

PEQ is not a traditional course. It is a practical journey where progress is demonstrated through real deliverables, not passive learning.

---

## 2. Why PEQ Exists

Many senior engineers reach a point where technical expertise is no longer enough to keep growing.

Modern product companies increasingly value engineers who can:

- understand user problems;
- reason about business impact;
- collaborate with product, design, data, and leadership;
- make pragmatic trade-offs;
- communicate clearly;
- use AI as a professional multiplier;
- help teams build the right product, not just build the product right.

PEQ exists to create a structured path for that evolution.

Its purpose is to help a strong technical engineer develop the mindset, habits, and evidence required to operate as a Product Engineer.

---

## 3. Who PEQ Is For

PEQ is initially designed for one primary player: César González, a Senior iOS Engineer based in Madrid who wants to evolve toward a more product-oriented engineering profile.

The system is optimized for someone who:

- already has strong technical foundations;
- benefits from clear structure;
- needs visible progress to maintain motivation;
- prefers short and medium-term goals over vague long-term plans;
- learns better through practical output than passive study;
- wants to grow without abandoning their engineering identity.

In the future, PEQ may be adapted for other software engineers with similar goals.

---

## 4. The Problem PEQ Solves

PEQ solves a common problem for senior engineers:

> “I know I need to become more product-oriented, but I do not know exactly what to practice, how to measure progress, or how to avoid abandoning the process.”

The challenge is not lack of ambition.

The challenge is lack of structure.

PEQ turns professional growth into a visible, measurable, and iterative system.

---

## 5. What PEQ Is Not

PEQ is not:

- a bootcamp;
- a university course;
- a collection of videos;
- a rigid twelve-month roadmap;
- a productivity system;
- a habit tracker;
- a replacement for real work experience;
- a game for entertainment;
- a system that rewards time spent instead of learning demonstrated.

PEQ should never become busywork.

Every activity must either build a skill, produce evidence, improve judgment, or support reflection.

---

## 6. How Learning Works in PEQ

Learning in PEQ happens through five loops:

### 1. Study

The player studies selected material such as books, articles, case studies, talks, or product examples.

### 2. Analyze

The player analyzes real products, features, decisions, and user flows.

### 3. Produce

The player creates concrete deliverables such as product analyses, RFCs, decision documents, hypotheses, experiments, and retrospectives.

### 4. Reflect

The player reviews what was learned, what was difficult, what was useful, and what should change.

### 5. Apply

The player connects learning back to real professional contexts, personal projects, or interview preparation.

PEQ values applied reasoning over passive consumption.

---

## 7. How Progress Is Measured

Progress is measured through evidence.

Evidence may include:

- completed product analyses;
- written hypotheses;
- RFCs;
- decision records;
- business reasoning;
- trade-off analysis;
- AI-assisted workflows;
- weekly reviews;
- boss battle responses;
- improved interview answers;
- real-world application at work or in side projects.

XP is used to make progress visible, but XP is not the ultimate goal.

The real goal is improved judgment.

---

## 8. What It Means to Complete a Season

A Season is complete when the player has demonstrated meaningful progress in the target skills of that Season.

Completion is not based only on finishing tasks.

A Season should be considered complete when there is enough evidence that the player can think, communicate, and decide differently than before.

For Season 1, success means the player can look at a feature and reason clearly about:

- the user problem;
- the business context;
- the product hypothesis;
- the success metric;
- the trade-offs;
- the simplest useful version;
- the role engineering should play in shaping the outcome.

---

## 9. Core Principles

### Principle 1: Evidence over intention

Good intentions do not count as progress. PEQ rewards demonstrated learning through concrete output.

### Principle 2: Quality over quantity

The system should not reward completing many shallow tasks. One thoughtful analysis is more valuable than five mechanical ones.

### Principle 3: Structure without rigidity

PEQ should provide enough structure to avoid ambiguity, but enough flexibility to adapt after each Season.

### Principle 4: Short feedback loops

The player should see progress every week. Motivation depends on visible movement.

### Principle 5: Real deliverables

Every important activity should produce something useful: a note, analysis, RFC, hypothesis, decision, review, or reusable insight.

### Principle 6: Product thinking before tooling

The system should be designed before the website, dashboard, or automation. Tools must serve the methodology, not define it.

### Principle 7: Do not reward time spent

Hours are not the main metric. Output, reasoning, and demonstrated skill matter more.

### Principle 8: Personal first, product later

PEQ is first designed to help one real person grow. Only after it works in practice should it be considered for public use or monetization.

### Principle 9: Engineering identity is preserved

PEQ does not try to turn engineers into Product Managers. It helps engineers become stronger product-minded technical contributors.

### Principle 10: Iterate like a product

PEQ itself should evolve through feedback, retrospectives, and versioning.
