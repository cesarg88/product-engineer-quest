# Skills

## Purpose

This document defines the four core skills trained in PEQ v0.1.

The goal is not to create a complex skill tree. The goal is to make clear what the player is practicing and how progress can be observed.

In PEQ v0.1, skills improve through quests, evidence, and weekly reflection.

---

## Skill 1: Product Thinking

### Definition

Product Thinking is the ability to reason about software from the perspective of user problems, product outcomes, hypotheses, metrics, and trade-offs.

A Product Engineer does not only ask:

> How do we build this?

They also ask:

> Why are we building this, who is it for, and how will we know if it worked?

### What this skill trains

- Understanding user problems.
- Identifying product hypotheses.
- Defining success metrics.
- Thinking in MVPs.
- Recognizing unnecessary complexity.
- Connecting engineering work to product outcomes.

### Signs of progress

The player is improving Product Thinking when they can:

- explain the user problem behind a feature;
- define a hypothesis before proposing a solution;
- identify one or two meaningful success metrics;
- suggest a simpler version of a feature;
- challenge a requirement without being negative;
- connect technical work to user value.

### Example quests

- Analyze why a product feature exists.
- Write the hypothesis behind a feature.
- Define success metrics for a product change.
- Compare two possible MVPs.
- Review a feature from Fever or PickOne through a product lens.

## Skill 2: Business Thinking

### Definition

Business Thinking is the ability to understand how product and engineering decisions affect the business.

A Product Engineer does not need to behave like a CFO or a founder, but should understand that every decision has cost, risk, opportunity, and expected value.

### What this skill trains

- Understanding business models.
- Connecting features to revenue, retention, conversion, or efficiency.
- Thinking about cost of delay.
- Understanding trade-offs between speed, quality, and impact.
- Recognizing when a technical investment supports business goals.

### Signs of progress

The player is improving Business Thinking when they can:

- explain why a feature may matter to the company;
- connect a product change to a business metric;
- reason about the cost of building versus not building;
- identify whether a technical improvement has business value;
- discuss prioritization beyond technical preference.

### Example quests

- Analyze how a feature could affect conversion.
- Identify the business goal behind a product decision.
- Write a trade-off between speed and quality.
- Explain how technical debt can affect product delivery.
- Analyze a company like Qonto, Fever, Revolut, or Wise from a business perspective.

## Skill 3: Product Leadership

### Definition

Product Leadership is the ability to influence product and technical decisions without relying on authority.

For an engineer, leadership means creating clarity, reducing uncertainty, improving decisions, and helping the team move toward better outcomes.

### What this skill trains

- Clear written communication.
- Stakeholder-aware reasoning.
- Constructive disagreement.
- Decision framing.
- Ownership beyond implementation.
- Explaining technical trade-offs to non-technical people.

### Signs of progress

The player is improving Product Leadership when they can:

- write a clear RFC or decision note;
- explain trade-offs without excessive technical detail;
- disagree with a proposal while offering alternatives;
- ask better questions in product discussions;
- communicate risks early;
- help a team converge on a decision.

### Example quests

- Write a short RFC.
- Rewrite a technical concern as a product trade-off.
- Prepare a recommendation for a PM.
- Simulate a disagreement with design or product.
- Reflect on a real work discussion and identify how it could have been improved.

## Skill 4: AI Engineering

### Definition

AI Engineering is the ability to use AI as a professional multiplier while keeping human judgment in control.

In PEQ, AI is not used only to go faster. It is used to think better, explore alternatives, improve communication, detect blind spots, and raise the quality of engineering and product work.

### What this skill trains

- Prompting for reasoning, not just output.
- Using AI to explore product hypotheses.
- Using AI to review documents.
- Using AI to compare technical options.
- Identifying AI limitations.
- Applying human judgment to AI-generated suggestions.

### Signs of progress

The player is improving AI Engineering when they can:

- design reusable AI workflows;
- use AI to improve a product analysis or RFC;
- identify weak AI suggestions;
- compare alternatives with AI support;
- use AI to prepare better questions;
- avoid blindly accepting AI output.

### Example quests

- Use AI to critique a product hypothesis.
- Use AI to generate edge cases for a feature.
- Use AI to review an RFC.
- Use AI to simulate PM, designer, or CTO feedback.
- Document an AI workflow that produced useful professional output.

## Skill Balance

Season 1 is mainly focused on Product Thinking.

Recommended emphasis:

- Product Thinking: 50%
- Product Leadership: 25%
- AI Engineering: 15%
- Business Thinking: 10%

Business Thinking is included, but it should not dominate Season 1.

The first goal is to stop thinking only as a builder and start thinking more clearly as a product-oriented engineer.

---

## Core Rule

A skill is not improved by reading about it.

A skill is improved when the player produces evidence that shows better reasoning, better decisions, or better communication.
